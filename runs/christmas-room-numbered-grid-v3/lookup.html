  1. Solve the Sudoku. Read row and column labels from its borders: row 1, column 2 is R1C2. Every originally blank cell has one lookup row.
  2. Find that cell in the lookup. Rows are grouped R1–R9. Read across to the column headed by its solved digit.
  3. Find the starting number in the drawing sector. Drawing sectors A–F / 1–6 are separate from Sudoku addresses.
  4. Follow the commands left to right. +6 means draw six consecutive-number connections. join 127 means draw one line directly to dot 127. If another + command follows, continue counting from 127. Do not lift at a join.
  5. Lift only at the end of the lookup entry. Tick the row, then continue to the next blank Sudoku cell.

Notation example: E3 / 305 · +8 · join 127 · +2 means start at 305 in sector E3, connect through 313, connect directly to 127, then 128 and 129. Lift. This example is not an answer.

Every dot has one number. Joining a numbered point lets strokes meet without moving junctions or adding gaps. All connections, including joins, remain short.
